# CubeSprite model training history

## About this document

This is a development history reconstructed from the author's surviving
training records and personal recollection. Some early experimental details
were not recorded completely and may have been forgotten. Approximate values
are identified as such. This document is intended to explain how the released
models evolved; it is not a complete reproducibility report or a controlled
ablation study.

## v2.1 High

v2.1 High follows a traditional AlphaZero-style architecture. It uses a
single-channel board representation, replaces the usual 2D convolutions with 3D
convolutions, and contains four residual blocks. It was originally designed to
be trained and used entirely on a CPU.

Throughout training, self-play used a conventional temperature schedule:
temperature 1 for the first 15 moves and 0 afterwards. The model received
approximately 32 hours of CPU training over about 50 iterations.

For roughly the first 30 iterations, its playing strength remained comparable
to a random policy, and a human could defeat it easily within the first 15
moves. Its strength then improved rapidly. It defeated the random policy and,
during the final 20 iterations, began to offer meaningful resistance to a
human player. Its strategic play was still limited, however, and its MCTS
behaviour and response time could be unusually complex or inconsistent.

The early training runs did not use a history window. A history window was
introduced only after iteration 40 and was set to 3. Virtual loss was not used
because no GPU-parallel training was involved. Based on later experiments with
other models, the author suspects that serial MCTS stayed relatively close to
the underlying search process and helped produce useful self-play data even at
only about 128 simulations. This remains an interpretation from experience,
not a controlled comparison.

## v2.2 Balance

The v2.2 codebase was reworked for GPU-accelerated training. The input changed
from a single view to a two-plane representation, and the rules were fixed to a
six-layer board. Removing upper positions that were rarely reached reduced the
input dimensionality.

The v2.2 family used fixed policy and value heads. An `8b256c` network—eight
residual blocks with 256 channels—was trained first as `v2.2_large`. After
approximately 200 iterations, a checkpoint around iteration 180 was selected
as a stable teacher. That model generated 1,000 self-play games with 1,600 MCTS
simulations. The games were divided across four warm-start iterations for the
smaller `6b224c` v2.2 Balance network, which has six residual blocks and 224
channels.

Balance was then trained through iteration 200 using 512 MCTS simulations for
self-play. After iteration 100, each evaluation against large contained 40
games, and Balance achieved a win rate above 50% in every such evaluation.

The experiments ran on an RTX 4090 with 16 vCPUs. Training large took about ten
days of wall-clock time, while Balance took about one week.

During large training, self-play used eight concurrent search threads and a
virtual-loss multiplier of 1.2. Larger virtual-loss settings were extremely
unstable: after 10–20 iterations, the policy could collapse, with short games
of only 7–10 moves growing to roughly 80–95% of the self-play data, after which
the run could no longer replace its best checkpoint.

For Balance, concurrency was reduced to four threads in an attempt to make
search behaviour closer to CPU training. This reduced self-play throughput but
produced a more stable training process.

## CubeSprite V3 and CubeSprite V3 mini

The CubeSprite V3 family is specialized for the gravity rules of the `5 × 5 ×
6` game. The policy output was reduced from the earlier 150-dimensional cell
space to the 25 playable columns. This encodes the effective action space
directly and substantially reduces the model and search overhead associated
with the output head.

Both CubeSprite V3 models began with a distillation warm start based on 2,000
self-play games generated by v2.2 Balance. During roughly iterations 5–20, the
teacher was phased out gradually rather than removed at once: the training pool
combined a proportion of pure student self-play with games played against the
teacher.

This was followed by approximately 180 iterations of teacher-protected
self-play. If an evaluation showed the student's win rate against the teacher
falling below 50%, the pipeline triggered a 40-game pulse of teacher matches
and added those games to the next iteration's data pool. Neither student beat
v2.2 Balance continuously throughout training; their evaluation win rates
fluctuated.

After both models reached iteration 200, the flagship model's training was
clearly more stable. CubeSprite V3 mini's latest best checkpoint at that stage
was iteration 162, while the flagship model went no more than 12 iterations
without refreshing its best checkpoint.

The mini model then received eight iterations of league-based reinforcement
learning with 800 games per iteration, followed by approximately 20 iterations
of opening-focused reinforcement learning. These stages were intended to
improve opening quality and strategic diversity. Self-play then continued to
iteration 260.

The flagship model did not show the same persistent opening weakness or fall
outside its history window because of a long failure to refresh its best
checkpoint. Its regular self-play training was therefore extended directly to
iteration 240, producing the released flagship checkpoint.

This part of the month-long experiment ran on an RTX 4090 with 20 vCPUs. Each
model required roughly ten days of total wall-clock training. Search used 512
MCTS simulations, four threads, and a virtual-loss multiplier of 1. The
temperature and exploration noise followed a staged schedule designed for 3D
Connect Four: lower exploration in the opening, higher exploration in the
middle game, and reduced exploration again in the late game.

The flagship model trained relatively steadily under this schedule. Its lower
opening exploration improved opening consistency and increased the amount of
useful long-game data. The mini model was less stable. In manual games against
its iteration-200 checkpoint, it was prone to strategic mistakes on the first
layer within the first 12 moves, often losing before its spatial understanding
could be meaningfully assessed. The opening-focused reinforcement-learning
stage reduced this problem, although it could still appear at higher
temperature settings.

Both released models provided a reasonable experience in informal human play.
This observation should not be interpreted as a calibrated human-strength
rating. Runtime and MCTS scaling evidence is stored under
[`test_results/`](test_results/).

## Released checkpoints and formats

| Model | Released checkpoint | Runtime format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CubeSprite V3 | Iteration 240 | ONNX in the desktop and Android applications |
| CubeSprite V3 mini | Iteration 260 | ONNX in the desktop and Android applications |
| v2.2 Balance | Stable v2.2 checkpoint | ONNX in the applications |
| v2.1 High | Legacy checkpoint at approximately 50 iterations | Adapted ONNX in the desktop application |

The public model package is intended to include the original PyTorch
`state_dict` checkpoints (`.pth`), a machine-readable manifest, conversion
information, and SHA-256 checksums. The applications continue to use ONNX
exports for deployment.

# 中文备份：模型训练回忆

> 以下内容根据现存训练记录和作者回忆整理。部分早期实验细节没有被完整记录，
> 也可能已经遗忘；近似数字均按回忆保留。本文用于说明发布模型的演变过程，
> 并不是完整的复现实验报告或严格的消融研究。

## v2.1 High

这是一个按照传统 AlphaZero 架构设计的单通道输入模型，将常见的 2D 卷积替换为
3D 卷积，并配置了 4 个残差块。这个模型最初被设计为完全在 CPU 上训练和使用。

训练全程采用传统 AlphaZero 温度策略：前 15 步温度为 1，之后为 0。最终进行了
约 32 小时的 CPU 训练，共约 50 个 Iterations。

在前约 30 个 Iterations 中，它的能力与随机策略相当，人类可以在前 15 步内轻松
击败它。此后模型迅速击败随机策略，并在最后约 20 个 Iterations 中得到显著提升，
开始能够与人类选手进行初步对抗。但是它的策略性仍然不足，MCTS 的表现可能较为
复杂，响应时间也比较异常。

早期训练没有配置 history window。直到 Iteration 40 之后才正式启用，且窗口值
只有 3。因为没有 GPU 并行训练，也没有配置虚拟损失。结合后来训练其他模型的
经验，我推测串行 MCTS 更接近搜索本身，并使模型在只有约 128 次 MCTS 搜索时仍能
产生质量较好的训练数据；但这只是经验判断，没有进行严格的并发对照实验。

## v2.2 Balance

为了更贴合 GPU 加速训练，v2.2 系列重构了代码仓库，将输入从单视图改为双视图，
同时把游戏规则固定为 6 layers，去掉高层空间中几乎不会使用的位置，以降低输入
维度。

该系列使用固定的策略头和价值头。首先训练 `8b256c` 的 v2.2 Large，即 8 个残差块、
每层 256 个通道。经过约 200 个 Iterations 后，选取 Iteration 180 左右的 checkpoint
作为较稳定的教师模型。随后由 Large 以 1,600 次 MCTS 搜索生成 1,000 局自对弈数据，
分成 4 个 Iterations，用于热启动 `6b224c` 的 v2.2 Balance，即 6 个残差块、每层
224 个通道。

此后 Balance 使用 512 次 MCTS 搜索进行自对弈，训练至 Iteration 200。Iteration
100 以后，每次对抗 Large 的评估均为 40 局，Balance 在每次评估中的胜率都超过
50%。

整个过程在 RTX 4090 和 16 vCPU 上完成。Large 的墙钟训练时间约为 10 天，Balance
约为一周。

Large 训练时采用 8 threads 并发和 1.2 倍虚拟损失。更高的虚拟损失设置表现得非常
不稳定：经过 10–20 个 Iterations 后可能发生策略坍缩，步数只有 7–10 的短局会增长
到自对弈数据的约 80%–95%，并且之后无法刷新 best checkpoint。

训练 Balance 时，并发进一步降低到 4 threads，以使搜索表现更接近 CPU 训练。
实际结果是自对弈速度有所降低，但训练过程更加稳定。

## CubeSprite V3 与 CubeSprite V3 mini

V3 系列针对 `5 × 5 × 6` 游戏及其重力规则进行了专项优化，将策略输出从原先的
150 维棋盘位置降低为 25 个实际可落子的列，直接表示有效动作空间，并显著减少输出
头和搜索相关的开销。

两个 CubeSprite V3 模型都先使用 v2.2 Balance 生成的 2,000 局自对弈数据进行蒸馏
热启动。在大约 Iterations 5–20 期间，教师模型没有被立即完全撤出；训练数据池按
一定比例混合学生模型自对弈，以及学生模型与教师模型对抗产生的数据。

随后进入约 180 个 Iterations 的教师保护自对弈。如果一次评估中学生模型对教师的
胜率低于 50%，则触发 40 局脉冲对抗，并将这些数据加入下一次迭代的数据池。两个
模型在整个训练过程中都不是持续战胜教师 v2.2 Balance，而是呈现胜率波动。

两个模型到达 Iteration 200 后，旗舰版的训练明显比 mini 版稳定。当时 mini 版最新
的 best checkpoint 仍是 Iteration 162；旗舰版则没有出现超过 12 个 Iterations
不刷新 best checkpoint 的情况。

随后 mini 版进行了 8 个 Iterations、每次 800 局的联赛对抗强化学习，又进行了约
20 个 Iterations 的 opening RL。这两个阶段旨在改善开局质量和策略多样性，之后
继续自对弈训练至 Iteration 260。

旗舰版没有出现持续的开局策略问题，也没有因长时间不刷新 best checkpoint 而超出
history window，因此直接延长常规训练至 Iteration 240，得到正式发布的旗舰模型。

这部分为期约一个月的实验在 RTX 4090 和 20 vCPU 上完成，两个模型各自的总墙钟
训练时间都在 10 天左右。训练全程采用 512 MCTS、4 threads 和虚拟损失系数 1。
同时为 3D Connect Four 设计了阶段性的温度与噪声：前期低探索、中期提高、后期再
降低。

旗舰版在该温度配置下训练较为稳定。前期低探索提高了开局稳定性，也增加了有效长局
数据。mini 版则不够稳定。我在人工对抗其 Iteration 200 checkpoint 时发现，它很
容易在前 12 手内于第一层发生策略性失误，导致开局失败，因而难以进一步检验其空间
理解能力。后来的 opening RL 缓解了这一问题，但在较高温度配置下仍可能出现。

两个模型都不是从头到尾持续战胜教师 v2.2 Balance，不过在人类对抗的非正式体验中
表现尚可。这不代表经过标定的人类强度评级；具体运行时和 MCTS scaling 数据见
[`test_results/`](test_results/)。
